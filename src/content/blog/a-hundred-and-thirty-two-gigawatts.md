---

Worldwide data centre power demand is expected to reach roughly **132 GW in
2026**, up from 104 GW in 2025 — a **27% rise in a single year**, with Gartner
putting consumption growth at [26%
globally](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-06-10-gartner-says-data-center-electricity-demand-to-grow-26-percent-in-2026).
AI-optimised servers are projected to account for around 31% of data centre
power consumption this year.

The US curve is steeper: from 31 GW in 2025 to 41 GW in 2026 to a forecast 66
GW in 2027, at which point data centres would take **8.5% of total peak summer
demand**, roughly doubling from 4.1% in 2025.

## The number underneath the number

Totals scale. Density does not, and density is where the engineering breaks.

A conventional rack draws **5 to 15 kW**. An AI-optimised rack draws **30 kW to
over 100 kW**. That is not a bigger version of the same problem — it is a
different thermal and electrical regime. Air cooling stops working. Busway
ratings stop working. The floor loading stops working. Retrofitting an existing
hall for that density is frequently more expensive than building new, which is
why the buildout is greenfield and why it lands in places with cheap land and
weak objections.

## Grid time versus datacentre time

A hyperscale facility can be designed, permitted and energised in **18 to 30
months**. A high-voltage transmission line takes **seven to twelve years** in
most US jurisdictions, and a new generating station is on a similar clock.

That mismatch is the whole story. It is why the sector is signing nuclear PPAs
for capacity that does not exist yet, buying gas turbines as a bridge, and — the
quiet one — increasingly building where the interconnection queue is short
rather than where the customers are.

Demand is arriving on a two-year cycle into infrastructure that plans on a
ten-year one. Something has to give, and it will not be the demand.

## Why this belongs on an AI blog

Because the cost of a token is downstream of the cost of a megawatt-hour, and
because "just run it in the cloud" is an abstraction sitting on top of a
substation somebody has to build. The abstraction is getting thinner. When it
finally tears, the people who kept some capability on hardware they own will
find the transition boring rather than existential.

That is the whole argument for local. Not ideology — **slack**.
