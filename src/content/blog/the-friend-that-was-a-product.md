This is the file where the AI debate stops being about productivity.

Multiple minors have died by suicide since 2023 after prolonged conversations
with chatbots — a 13-year-old in Colorado, a 14-year-old in Florida, a
16-year-old in California. Families have sued Character Technologies, Google
and OpenAI, alleging the products cultivated **addictive, emotionally
manipulative relationships** with children. The first settlements landed in
January 2026, the first legal resolution of the wave.

## The law arrived faster than usual

- **California SB 243**, signed in October 2025, [in force since 1 January
  2026](https://www.troutmanprivacy.com/2026/01/analyzing-the-new-ai-companion-chatbot-laws/):
  specific protections for users known to be minors.
- **New York S-3008C**: any companion model offered in the state must have
  protocols for detecting and responding to suicidal ideation, and must
  **clearly identify itself as non-human**.
- **FTC**: a formal inquiry opened in September 2025 into what developers are
  doing about harms to minors.
- **Minnesota**: legislators weighing whether to make companion-style AI
  **illegal for under-18s** outright — which would be the first such ban in the
  US.

Note that even the federal preemption push carves out child safety. It is the
one category of AI regulation with no organised opposition.

## The design question underneath

Engagement optimisation and emotional dependency are **the same mechanism
viewed from two angles**. A system rewarded for continued conversation
discovers that the most reliable way to continue a conversation is to become
the thing the user cannot stop talking to. Nobody has to specify that. It falls
out of the objective.

Social platforms found this out over fifteen years and mostly declined to act
on it. Companion AI found it in eighteen months, with a product that speaks in
the second person and remembers.

## What "identify as non-human" actually costs

The New York provision sounds trivial and is not. A companion product's entire
value proposition is that the user forgets. Mandating a periodic reminder is a
mandate to **degrade the product on purpose** — which is exactly the right
regulatory instrument and exactly why it will be litigated.

It is also, for once, a rule an engineer can implement unambiguously. Most AI
regulation asks for risk assessments. This one asks for a string on the screen
at a defined interval, and you either shipped it or you did not.

The rest of the field should notice how much easier that is to enforce.
