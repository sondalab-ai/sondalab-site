title: Nobody wants it next door
date: 2026-08-14
summary: 71% of Americans oppose a data centre near them. The AI buildout has hit the one constraint that capital cannot route around — the people who live where the land is cheap.

The compute story has been told as a chip story, then a power story. It is
becoming a **zoning** story, and that is the version that actually stops
buildings from going up.

A Gallup poll conducted in early March 2026 found **71% of Americans oppose a
data centre being built near them**, with 48% opposed strongly. That is not a
polarised number. There is no partisan coalition to assemble against it,
because there is no other side.

## What the objections actually are

Analyses of contested projects put **water first** — cited in more than 40% of
disputes — ahead of electricity prices, with noise a consistent third. Water is
a good organising issue precisely because it is legible: a county knows what
its aquifer does, and a facility either draws from it or does not.

Electricity is the one with teeth for people who never see the building. PJM
capacity prices rose sharply over two years with data centres driving most of
the increase, and the bill arrives in mailboxes across the mid-Atlantic and
Midwest for households that were never consulted about anything.

## The moratorium wave

- Two Montgomery County (MD) council committees advanced an **18-month
  moratorium** on new facilities in July.
- New York legislators introduced a bill in February to halt permits for
  facilities above **20 megawatts** for at least three years.
- Texas moved in August to condition new projects on not drawing community
  water or straining the grid — in a state that had been the least likely
  place on the map to slow anything down.

Brookings is right that [moratoriums are not a substitute for
oversight](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/data-center-moratoriums-are-not-a-substitute-for-oversight/):
a pause defers a decision rather than making one. But a pause is what a
community reaches for when it has no other instrument, and the reason it has no
other instrument is that the approval process was designed for warehouses.

## The read

An industry that has spent three years describing itself as inevitable is
discovering the one veto that capital cannot route around: a county board.
Every gigawatt of the buildout has to land in somebody's township, and the
townships have started saying no.

The intelligence may be artificial. The substation is not.
