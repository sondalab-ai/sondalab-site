---
title: A thousand robots, mostly watching
date: 2026-05-22
summary: Humanoids reached real factory floors this year. No manufacturer has sustained a deployment above the low hundreds of units, and most robots come with a supervisor.
---

Humanoid robots crossed from demo to deployment in 2026, and the reality is
more interesting than either the hype or the backlash.

Tesla has an estimated **1,000 to 1,200 Optimus units** across Fremont and Giga
Texas, with **zero external sales**. Musk described them on an earnings call as
"primarily for learning and data collection rather than performing productive
tasks" — which is a remarkably honest thing to say about a fleet that size, and
it went almost entirely unquoted.

Figure completed an eleven-month pilot with BMW at Spartanburg. Boston Dynamics
began commercial leasing of the electric Atlas. And the load-bearing sentence
in the whole sector: as of mid-2026, [no humanoid from any manufacturer has
been deployed above the low hundreds of units in a sustained commercial
environment](https://www.technology.org/2026/07/18/humanoid-robots-in-2026-what-is-actually-deployed/).

## What they are actually doing

The tasks are real — battery module assembly, parts kitting, final assembly
assist — and they are on live production lines rather than in isolated
demonstration cells. That is a genuine milestone and it should not be
minimised.

But the deployment pattern is consistent everywhere: **a robot paired with a
human supervisor** who handles exceptions and reprograms when the task drifts.
The robot does the repetitive, well-specified centre of the task. The human
does the edges.

## Where you have heard this before

That is precisely the shape of every agent deployment in software. The model
handles the well-specified centre; a person handles the exceptions; the
economics turn entirely on **what fraction of the work is centre versus edge**,
and that fraction is domain-specific and almost never what the demo implies.

Robotics just makes it physical and therefore impossible to hand-wave. You can
ship a chatbot that fails 15% of the time and call it a beta. A manipulator that
drops a battery module 15% of the time is a stop-work order.

## The honest timeline

Manufacturing is the easy case: structured environment, known objects, repeated
motion, and a facility you control. It is taking a decade and hundreds of
millions of dollars per company to make that case work at hundreds of units.

Anyone extrapolating from a Fremont assembly line to a household in three years
is selling something. The gap between a factory floor and a kitchen is not
scale. It is that the factory was designed for the robot, and the kitchen was
designed for you.
